**What happened.** The Bank of Japan raised its target interest rate from 1% to 1.25%, the highest level since 1995, in an effort to curb inflation tied to the war in Iran. The move follows similar tightening from the US Federal Reserve and the European Central Bank as central banks respond to rising energy prices worldwide.

**Potential implications.** This is a bigger deal for Japan than the 25 basis points suggests. For three decades the BoJ's default setting was near-zero or negative rates to fight deflation; now it's tightening into what looks like a geopolitically-driven energy shock, the same playbook as everyone else. The awkward part is that rate hikes are a demand-side tool being used against a supply-side problem — an oil shock from a war doesn't go away because borrowing costs rise, it just gets more expensive to borrow while you wait it out. Watch two things: Japan's government debt (among the highest in the world relative to GDP) gets meaningfully costlier to service at 1.25% than at 1%, and a rising Japanese rate narrows the gap with the Fed and ECB, which could unwind the yen carry trade that's quietly funded a lot of global risk-taking for years. None of this is catastrophic on its own, but it's the kind of thing that turns a regional war into a global financial story.

### Strangers Carried Each Other Through New York's Darkest Night

When the Northeast blackout of 1965 plunged New York into darkness, Lynn Lowin was among 800,000 people trapped in the city's subway system with no idea when the lights would return. For six hours, strangers who had never spoken to one another began sharing flashlights, food, and reassurance, guiding frightened commuters through pitch-black tunnels and stairwells by touch and voice alone. Lowin, now in her senior years, recently reached out to Good News Network to finally tell the story that has stayed with her for six decades. What she remembers isn't the fear of being stuck underground, but the quiet decency of people who had every excuse to panic and instead chose to look after one another.
